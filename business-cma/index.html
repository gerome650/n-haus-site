Business CMA · Comparative market analysis

See how your business compares, and what to fix first.

A Business CMA is a written report. It compares what customers can see of your business with five to ten direct local competitors, shows the evidence behind each finding, and ends with three actions in priority order.

Who it is for

One business, one service, one market.

The first release is built for owner-operated local service businesses: one location or service area, one main service, and one type of customer per report. A business with several locations needs a separate analysis for each.

The report covers what a customer can see from the outside: your website and service pages, your public listing, public reviews, and the ways to contact or book you. We inspect public pages only. We do not submit forms, place test calls or contact your competitors.

It is not a business valuation, an SEO score or a revenue forecast. We check that a CMA fits your business before any purchase.

The report

Nine sections, the same in every report.

An evidence appendix follows, listing sources, collection dates, exclusions and assumptions.

SectionWhat it tells youNo.

Business snapshot

Who you are, what you sell, where you serve and which channels we looked at. It also lists what we could not find out.

01

Comparable businesses

Five to ten direct competitors for the same customer, the reason each was chosen, and who was left out. If fewer than five qualify, the report says so instead of stretching the comparison.

02

Market benchmark

Your figures beside theirs: the raw values, how many businesses each comparison covers, and the dates they were collected.

03

Where you are ahead

Strengths the evidence supports, and what to keep doing. If none are evidenced, the report says that.

04

Where competitors are ahead

Gaps the evidence supports, how serious each one is, and how confident we are in it.

05

Revenue risk and opportunity

Which step of the customer's path is affected and why it matters. Money figures appear only when you supply the numbers behind them, and then as labeled scenarios.

06

Top three actions

What to do first, the reason, who owns it, the first step, and how you will know it worked.

07

Repair options

Ways to carry out each action: doing it yourself, using another provider, or a scoped NHAUS repair where one applies.

08

Next 30 days

A dated plan with owners, dependencies and what to measure.

09

Review and evidence

A person checks every report.

A reviewer checks the evidence, the choice of competitors and each recommendation before a report is released. The findings are meant to be honest even when they lead to no sale.

Every claim has a source
Each finding links to what was observed and the date it was observed.
Unknown stays unknown
If something could not be checked, the report says not checked. It is never scored as a failure.
No guaranteed revenue
The CMA describes what customers can see. It does not forecast your revenue, and no repair comes with a promised result.
Advice is not ranked by what we earn
Actions are ordered by severity, urgency, ease and strength of evidence. What NHAUS would earn from a repair plays no part. A report can end with nothing to buy.

Repairs

A repair is a separate purchase.

Buying a CMA does not commit you to anything else. Every action in the report comes with a way to do it yourself, and you are free to use another provider.

Where NHAUS can do the work, a repair is one scoped job tied to a specific action in your report, with its own price and a test for when it is done. Nothing starts because it appears in a report. Repair scopes and prices are not published yet.

When the evidence calls for it, a repair can include work from the Production Studio, such as a film or a piece of content. The studio also takes commissions on its own, with no CMA required.
